01

Where the prototype stands today

beatbox is a striking, opinionated player shell — and almost none of a marketplace yet. That's the honest starting line, and it's a good one: the hardest thing to fake (a point of view) is already here.

What's real: a skewed cassette-deck SVG layout with hand-drawn hairline controls, a working HTML5 audio transport (play/pause, prev/next, volume, elapsed time), a list ⇄ carousel toggle, a light/dark theme with a genuine acid-green identity, and a local upload form that pushes a track into an in-memory array.

What isn't there — and what a marketplace is actually made of:

No backend / persistence No accounts or auth No checkout / payment No licensing model No waveform preview No search / tags / filter No messaging No per-producer store

Uploads currently live only in beatBank in the browser and vanish on refresh (URL.createObjectURL blobs). Every item below is really the question: what has to exist server-side before any of this counts.

02

The market you'd be entering

Selling beats online is a mature, consolidated space. The winner isn't the prettiest player — it's whoever combines a built-in buyer audience with frictionless licensing + checkout. Two incumbents dominate, with a tier of leaner challengers underneath competing on take rate and control.

Platform Model Commission Its edge Its weakness
BeatStars Marketplace + storefront ~12% fee Largest built-in audience; licensing & automation baked in You pay for that reach; less brand control
Airbit Storefront-first 0% + PSP fees Higher margins; strong analytics; SEO for your own store Smaller native audience; 50-track cap on entry tier
Traktrain Curated marketplace ~0% on MP3 Curated trap/hip-hop crowd; taste-driven No negotiation / counter-offers; narrow genre
BeatPass Subscription + exclusive varies Negotiated exclusives & custom beats between users Newer, smaller network
Self-host / Soundee Your own store lowest Full control & margin Zero audience — you bring all traffic

Sources: BeatPass, Feedtracks, SlimeGreenBeats, Audio Assemble platform comparisons; UnitedMasters & Orphiq licensing guides (2025–26).

The one mechanic that runs the whole market: tiered licensing

Nearly every sale is a license, not a file transfer. Producers stack tiers — the difference between them is what file you get, how far you can use it, and whether others can still buy it after you:

  • Non-exclusive (lease) — cheap ($20–50), sold to unlimited buyers, MP3/WAV, capped use. This is where the volume money is: one popular beat leased 50× at $50 = $2,500, far more than a single exclusive.
  • Premium / trackout lease — mid-price, stems included, higher usage caps.
  • Exclusive — ownership transfers, beat is pulled from the store. $200–500 for a bedroom producer; $2k–10k+ with real placements.

The fork: two viable positions, and they pull apart

One thing is settled — you cannot out-audience BeatStars and shouldn't try. But there are two credible answers to what beatbox is instead, and they imply different products. This is the decision everything else hangs off, so it's laid out rather than assumed.

Path A · Design-forward storefront

A beautiful, low-take home for one producer's own catalog — the Airbit shape. The wedge is the thing incumbents are ugly at: identity and feel.

  • For: one-sided, so it works with zero network. Ships far sooner. Your aesthetic is the product.
  • Against: you bring all the traffic. Ceiling is your own audience.
  • Needs: storefront, licensing, checkout. No messaging required.

Path B · Negotiation marketplace

Compete where the market is genuinely weak: the human transaction. Traktrain has no negotiation at all; BeatPass only gestures at it. Offers, counter-offers, custom briefs.

  • For: a real differentiator, not a nicer skin. Exclusives and custom work are where the money and relationships form.
  • Against: two-sided — needs producers and buyers before it does anything. Classic cold-start.
  • Needs: everything in Path A, plus identity, threads, offer state.

How to decide

The stages below are sequenced so you don't have to choose yet — Path A's build is a strict subset of Path B's. Build the storefront, and the fork stays open until the correspondence stage. Decide then, on evidence: if the inquiry button gets used, that's Path B telling you it's real.

03

UX findings — what buyers expect

Across the platforms buyers use daily, a handful of patterns are effectively table stakes. Each maps to a decision for beatbox.

Waveform preview is the single biggest gap

Every modern beat player (BeatPass, Airbit, the WaveformPlayer-style tools) renders a clickable waveform with instant seek. Buyers audition by scrubbing to the drop — a plain play button feels a decade old. Your transport is solid; give it a waveform and click-to-seek and it instantly reads as current.

The persistent play bar

Playback must survive navigation. A buyer browsing a catalog should keep hearing the current beat while they scroll, filter, and open other pages — a global bar, not a player that stops when the view changes. Your carousel/list toggle already hints at this; formalize one persistent transport that outlives view switches.

License options must be crystal-clear at the point of sale

The recurring UX failure in this space is murky terms. The best flows show, on each beat, exactly what file, what usage, what price per tier — before checkout, in plain language. Clarity here converts; ambiguity kills trust.

Search, tags & filter are how buyers actually shop

Buyers arrive with intent: a mood, a BPM, a key, an artist to sound like. Tagging (genre, BPM, key, mood) plus filter/search is the primary navigation of a catalog — the carousel is a lovely front door but a poor filing cabinet. Your beat-data.js schema already anticipates this; it just needs the fields and the filter UI.

Keep the checkout flow short

Conversion rises the shorter the path from hear it → pick license → pay. Cart optional; one-beat instant checkout should be possible.

Tagged previews — or you're giving the product away

Every commercial platform streams watermarked previews: a producer voice tag dropped every ~15 seconds over the audition file, with the clean version released only after purchase. Without it, the preview is the deliverable and there is no reason to pay. Your player currently serves the raw file straight from banks/<bank>/<file>. This is the one item on this page that is load-bearing for revenue existing at all.

Two risks specific to your build

The fixed viewBox="0 0 100 100" won't survive mobile. A large share of beat browsing happens on phones, and the layout's coordinates are absolute. The SVG identity is worth protecting — but it needs a responsive strategy that keeps the character without the rigid grid, and that's easier to design now than to retrofit later.

The transport is invisible to keyboards and screen readers. Hit detection in transport.js is coordinate math against click position, not focusable DOM elements — so play, prev and next can't be tabbed to or announced. Acceptable in an art piece; a genuine accessibility and legal problem once money changes hands.

On your questions — tagging & notes

Tagging: yes, essential — genre, BPM, key, mood. It's core navigation, not a nice-to-have. Notes: yes, but scope them. Private buyer notes (saved/bookmarked beats — your ★ already exists) are cheap and useful. Public "notes" blur into reviews/comments; defer those until there's a community to moderate.

04

Your three mechanisms — how to actually build them

Trading mechanism

Don't build "trading" as barter — the market's trade is licensing. Implement it as: each beat carries a set of license tiers (lease / premium / exclusive), each with a price, a deliverable (MP3 / WAV / stems), and terms text. "Sold exclusively" flips the beat to unlisted. That is the trade.

Payment mechanism

Ko-fi is the right instinct for speed but the wrong shape for a licensed catalog — it's tip/single-product-first and won't hand back a signed license PDF or per-track terms. Realistic options:

OptionGood forCostVerdict
Ko-fi ShopFastest launch, no backend~0–5%Fine for a v0 "buy" button; no license automation
Stripe CheckoutReal catalog, licenses, control~2.9% + 30¢The right long-term core — single seller
Stripe ConnectPaying other producers~2.9% + 30¢Required for Path B; handles splits, payouts, tax
Gumroad / Lemon SqueezyDigital delivery + license keys, low ops~5–10%Great middle ground; delivery handled for you

Recommendation: Ko-fi or Gumroad button now to prove someone will pay; migrate to Stripe once tiered licensing and gated file delivery exist. The one caveat: if you land on Path B, you need Stripe Connect, not plain Checkout — the moment money is owed to someone who isn't you, splits, payouts and tax paperwork stop being optional. Worth knowing before the migration, not during it.

Correspondence mechanism

This is your differentiator — Traktrain notably lacks negotiation, and exclusive deals are where relationships (and real money) form. Build it in two stages: first an inquiry/offer button per beat ("make an offer" → sends producer a message), then lightweight threaded messaging. The envelope icon already in your sidebar is the natural home for an inbox.

05

What to build, in order

Sequenced so each stage is independently shippable and each unlocks the next. The gate on everything is the same: a backend.

Build now

foundation

Turn the prototype into something that remembers and can take money.

  • Backend + database — persist beats, uploads, accounts (Supabase/Firebase is enough)
  • Real file storage for audio + artwork (replaces blob URLs)
  • License tiers on each beat (lease / premium / exclusive + terms)
  • Waveform preview with click-to-seek + one persistent play bar
  • Tagged/watermarked preview streams — clean files only after purchase
  • A single payment button (Ko-fi or Gumroad) to prove willingness to pay

Next

a real store

Make it navigable and make the sale clean.

  • Tags + search + filter (genre, BPM, key, mood)
  • Stripe checkout with gated file delivery + auto license PDF
  • Per-producer store pages (the Airbit-style wedge)
  • Saved / bookmarked beats tied to accounts (your ★, persisted)
  • Responsive layout strategy — the fixed viewBox, solved deliberately
  • Focusable, keyboard-operable transport controls
  • "Make an offer" inquiry button per beat → producer inbox (the fork)

Later

network effects

Only once there are producers and buyers to connect.

  • Threaded messaging / negotiation on exclusives
  • Producer analytics (plays, conversion)
  • Public reviews / comments (needs moderation)
  • Playlists, collections, discovery/curation
  • Subscription tier for buyers (BeatPass-style)
